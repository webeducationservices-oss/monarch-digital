AI for Restaurant Operations

AI for restaurant operations, from the host stand to the back office

Each assistant has one clear job and works from your own data.

Operations evaluations

An AI read of your service data and reviews that points to where covers and time slip away, so you know what to fix first.

Concierge and host help

A restaurant chatbot that answers availability questions from your real book, plus a host assistant that greets parties, looks up guests, proposes tables, and works by voice.

Server briefs

A greeting written the moment a party sits, plus quick answers to questions like "what do they drink?"

Sections and scheduling

Drafts of server sections and weekly schedules, plus help covering call-outs, approved by a manager before anything is published.

Kitchen pacing analyst

A read-only look at ticket times and pacing that shows where the kitchen fell behind on a busy night.

Paperwork readers

A menu import that turns the kitchen's dish file into a training menu, a flyer reader that turns a PDF into a draft event, and applicant summaries where every point is backed by a quote from the application.

Ask Your Data

Ask a plain question and get an answer from your own numbers

A read-only assistant answers questions across your restaurant's data, like how Saturday's turn times compared to last month or which regulars have a birthday this week. It can draft the follow-up email too, and a person reads it and hits send.

AI Marketing for Restaurants

AI marketing for restaurants, in your voice and on your approval

AI marketing for restaurants works when someone who knows your guests has the final say. Our assistants draft posts, review replies, emails, and ad copy from your menu, events, and brand voice. You or your manager reads each one, edits what you like, and approves it before it goes out.

It plugs into the rest of our restaurant marketing work, so your website, Google Business Profile, and social stay in step.

  • Social posts and blog drafts built from what's happening at your restaurant this week
  • Review replies drafted in your voice, ready for a manager's OK
  • Email drafts for events, holidays, and regulars
  • Ad copy options for your campaigns, reviewed before they run
AI Phone Answering

Restaurant AI phone answering, set up carefully

An AI receptionist for restaurants can take pressure off the host stand during a rush, and it can frustrate callers when it's set up poorly. We evaluate the options with you, test them against your real calls, and set them up with clear handoffs to your team. These are the checks we start with.

  • Hours, address, and parking answered correctly
  • Availability read from your real book
  • Booking requests passed to a person to confirm
  • Allergy and menu questions routed to your staff
  • One easy step to reach a person
  • Wording that sounds like your restaurant
  • Every call logged for managers to review
  • A clear fallback when it can't help

Thirteen AI assistants at a private dining club in Vail

Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does AI for a restaurant cost?
The assessment is free. Beyond that, every engagement is scoped to your restaurant, so there's no price list. You'll get a written plan with priorities, budget, and timeline, and nothing starts until you approve it.
How long does it take to set up an AI assistant?
It depends on the assistant and the information it needs. Your plan lays out the timeline, and you review each assistant with real examples before your team relies on it.
Does it work with my POS?
Our assistants work alongside your POS. They read the systems we build for you, like reservations, guest files, schedules, and menus, plus the documents you share. Your POS keeps handling orders and payments the way it does today.
Will the AI ever book a table or post something on its own?
No. A person confirms anything that books, publishes, or sends. Assistants answer questions, draft, and suggest, and someone on your team approves the action.
What do I need to provide?
Access to what each assistant should work from, such as menu files, your handbook, house rules, and brand voice, plus a manager to review drafts while we tune it. We do the work. You guide the process.
Does this work for one restaurant or a group?
Both. A single restaurant might start with review replies and server briefs. A group can roll the same assistants out to every location, with one standard for how teams use them.
