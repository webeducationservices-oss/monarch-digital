Restaurant Operations Consultant

Find the Money Already in Your P&L

A good restaurant operations consultant looks for money you're making and losing in the same night: tables that sit too long between courses, wait quotes that send walk-ins down the street, labor that runs heavy on slow shifts, and dishes that sell well but barely earn.

Many operators target a prime cost around 60 percent of sales, and every point above it comes out of profit. We find the leaks with an AI operations evaluation and a walkthrough, then fix them with the tools your team uses every shift. We do the work. You guide the process.

What We Look At

What a Restaurant Operations Consultant Should Measure

Food and beverage consulting and restaurant cost control, grounded in how your shifts actually run.

Pacing and Table Turns

How long each table takes from seat to check, where courses stall, and whether the book is slamming the kitchen at 7:30.

Wait-Time Accuracy

Quotes that run long lose walk-ins, and quotes that run short frustrate guests. We compare quoted waits to actual waits and tighten them night by night.

Labor Targets

Labor by daypart and by role, matched to your covers, so you staff up for the rush and cut early when the room slows.

Prime Cost

Food, beverage, and labor together, the number that decides whether a busy month was a good month. We track it weekly and show you where it moved.

Menu Engineering

Every dish sorted by popularity and profit into stars, plowhorses, puzzles, and dogs, with a plan to price, place, and train servers to sell each one.

SOPs and Routines

Written standards for opening, pre-shift, side work, and checkout, so every shift runs the same way no matter who's on the schedule.

Pacing in Practice

Pacing and Wait Times Your Team Can See

Findings matter most when the team can act on them during service. For a private dining club in Vail, Colorado, we built pacing into the tools: a cap of 15 covers per 15 minutes, rest time between seatings, and a kitchen screen with a clock on every seated table and a pacing line.

Walk-ins get live wait quotes and a one-tap text when their table is ready, and a daily report shows how accurate the quotes were. A read-only AI pacing analyst helps the kitchen see where service slowed down.

  • Pacing caps by 15-minute window
  • Live wait quotes with a daily accuracy report
  • A clock on every seated table in the kitchen
  • Turn time reporting for every service

Routines and Reporting

Fixes stick when they're built into the shift. We write your SOPs and set up a shift plan with pre-shift, mid-shift, and checkout views and a printed day sheet. Servers close out with their side work listed, and station prep sheets keep your best sellers from getting 86'd early.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a restaurant operations consultant cost?
Engagements are scoped to each restaurant, so there's no set price list. The AI assessment is free and shows where to look first. After the walkthrough, you get a written plan with priorities, budget, and timeline, and you approve it before anything starts.
How long before we see changes?
Some fixes can start at the next pre-shift, like a tighter side-work list or a pacing cap. Software, training, and scheduling roll out in phases on the timeline in your plan, and we stay on for support after launch.
Do you work with our POS and the vendors we already have?
Yes. Everything we build runs alongside your POS, and we keep the tools and vendors that are working for you. When something needs to change, we coordinate with your current vendors or bring in our network.
What do you need from us?
Recent P&Ls or prime cost numbers, cover counts by daypart, a sample schedule, your menu with recipe costs if you have them, and your handbook and SOPs. Plus time for the walkthrough, on site or virtual.
Is the AI evaluation the same as a restaurant audit?
It's the first step of one. The AI reads your answers, your Google listing and reviews, and your website, and flags where to look. The walkthrough goes deeper into your numbers, your floor, and your kitchen, and your written plan comes from both.
Do you work with single restaurants or multi-location groups?
Both. For an independent restaurant, we focus on the few changes worth the most. For a group, we compare locations side by side, find what your best unit does well, and write it into the SOPs and training for every location.
